Who this course is for

Oncologists, surgeons, gynaecologists, family physicians, internists and trainees increasingly receive genetic reports and meet families at high risk. This course gives them the working knowledge to decide when to test, how to read a result and when to refer.

Lessons are written to current guidance and peer-reviewed literature, and cite what they rest on.

Questions it answers

  • Which patients should be referred for oncogenetic counselling?
  • How are sequence variants classified by pathogenicity?
  • What does a BRCA1 or BRCA2 result change in surveillance and treatment?
  • How is Lynch syndrome identified through tumour screening?

Course faculty

Prof. Eitan Friedman, MD, PhD

Medical geneticist · Professor Emeritus, Tel Aviv University · Founding faculty

Four decades in medicine, cancer genetics and medical education. Prof. Friedman founded the Oncogenetics Unit at Sheba Medical Center and directed it from 1995 to 2021, established the Meirav High-Risk Clinic for women at inherited risk of breast and ovarian cancer, and today directs the oncogenetics service at Assuta Medical Center in Tel Aviv.

His research — much of it on BRCA1 and BRCA2 — has centered on translating inherited cancer risk into practical prevention, screening and early detection. Alongside it he has taught physicians and the public for years, through university teaching, professional courses, children's books and television.
